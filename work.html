Featured project

Radar Viewer & analysis tool

A web-based tool I built that the team now uses for all post-operation analysis. It brings every data source for an operation into one place, from NEXRAD and in-house radar to proprietary atmospheric data such as drone sensors.

I release it in versions and maintain automated Python and JavaScript test suites so analysts can count on it during the season.

Featured project

3-D mission replay

Radar data on its own is hard to read for anyone who doesn't look at it every day. The mission replay puts an entire operation into one three-dimensional scene, so a scientist, a client, or a prospective partner can watch seeding material being released, see the resulting signature develop and drift downwind, and follow the precipitation it produced.

Built with Three.js and Python from radar volumes, analyst-confirmed signature polygons, drone GPS telemetry, balloon soundings, ensemble QPE, and SRTM terrain. It covers 16 operations across four program areas and is being extended to visualize prospective operations in new domains.

  • Replay any operation at adjustable speed, or follow a guided cinematic tour.
  • A mission log tracks each seeding wave and the signature it produced.
  • Winds aloft and running precipitation totals update with the replay.
  • Toggle 3-D echoes, radar layers, accumulation, and wind vectors.

What I've built & led

QPE & validation framework

Developed Rainmaker's first quantitative precipitation estimation framework and the end-to-end radar validation pipeline around it. The pipeline identifies seeding signatures, isolates the precipitation enhancement, and quantifies it. I coded the whole pipeline, including customizable composite reflectivity generation and a 27-member QPE ensemble.

Season operations

Lead radar analyst for the 2025–2026 Pendleton, OR field season. I identified 55 seeding signatures across 52 intensive observation periods and wrote more than 40 validation reports, each quantifying the precipitation produced and the atmospheric and operational variables behind the outcome.

Automated reporting

I'm leading the work to automate validation reports across several program areas. The pipeline pulls MRMS, GOES-18, HRRR and GFS fields, ceilometer data, soundings, and drone telemetry, combines them with seeding flight logs and candidate signatures, and assembles a draft report that analysts check and finalize.

Season analysis & publication

First author of a paper on the 2025–2026 season, now in revision at Atmosphere. It documents 71 radar-observed precipitation enhancements across all program areas, with a bulk statistical analysis of how their properties relate to atmospheric conditions, to inform how future operations are designed. Early results were presented at the 2026 AMS Summit.

Communicating results

I work with business development and brand/comms to turn scientific results into client-facing figures, validation summaries, and proposal material, and have presented to state water-resource agencies, internal stakeholders, and academic conferences.

QPE in practice

The 27-member Ze–R ensemble running through one IOP. Composite reflectivity and instantaneous precipitation rate (A, B) build into total QPE (C). Inside the analyst-drawn signature polygon, the ensemble gives a range of integrated water volume over time (D), not a single number. Accumulations like these are the core quantitative piece of each validation report.

Other data I regularly combine with the radar: MRMS, GOES-18 satellite products, HRRR and GFS model guidance, ASOS and SNOTEL observations, ceilometers, and in-situ measurements from drones and balloons.
