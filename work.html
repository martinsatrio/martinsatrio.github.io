Featured project

Radar Viewer & analysis tool

A web-based tool I built that the team now uses for all post-operation analysis. It brings every data source for an operation into one place, from NEXRAD and in-house radar to proprietary atmospheric data such as drone sensors.

I release it in versions and maintain automated Python and JavaScript test suites so analysts can count on it during the season.

  • Draw polygons to isolate seeding signatures and track them from volume to volume.
  • Run the QPE workflow on those polygons directly.
  • Open terrain-aware 2-D and 3-D cross sections.
  • Export figures ready for reports.

What I've built & led

QPE & validation framework

Developed Rainmaker's first quantitative precipitation estimation framework and the end-to-end radar validation pipeline around it. The pipeline identifies seeding signatures, isolates the precipitation enhancement, and quantifies it. I coded the whole pipeline, including customizable composite reflectivity generation and a 27-member QPE ensemble.

Season operations

Lead radar analyst for the 2025–2026 Pendleton, OR field season. I identified 55 seeding signatures across 52 intensive observation periods and wrote more than 40 validation reports over the season.

Automated reporting

I'm leading the work to automate validation reports across several program areas. The system combines public and proprietary data on atmospheric conditions, seeding flights, and candidate signatures into one report that analysts can check and verify.

3-D mission replay

Built a tool that replays a complete IOP in 3-D, showing drone tracks, radar fields, seeding-signature polygons, and balloon flight paths together. I'm now extending it to visualize hypothetical scenarios when planning operations in new areas.

Season analysis & publication

Leading a first-author paper on the 2025–2026 season. It includes a bulk statistical analysis of seeding signatures and how their properties relate to atmospheric conditions, with the goal of informing how future operations are designed. Early results were presented at the 2026 AMS Summit.

Communicating results

Presented results to internal stakeholders, to external customers including state water-resource agencies, and at academic conferences.

QPE in practice

Rain rate and accumulated precipitation from the Pendleton, OR (KPDT) WSR-88D, computed with the QPE framework. Accumulations like these are the core quantitative piece of each validation report.

Other data I regularly combine with the radar: HRRR model guidance, ASOS and SNOTEL observations, satellite imagery, and in-situ measurements from drones and balloons.
